End of module assignment · synopsis

Pima diabetes analysis

I analysed 768 records to describe the sample, compare groups and examine relationships between diabetes status and the recorded predictors. I treated implausible zeros in clinical measurements as missing, while retaining valid zero pregnancies. I used descriptive statistics, group tests, correlation, linear regression and logistic regression.

My reported logistic model used glucose, BMI and age, with 77.0% accuracy, 56.8% sensitivity and 87.9% specificity. The gap between sensitivity and specificity matters: the overall accuracy conceals many missed positive cases. Complete-case cleaning also reduced the linear regression sample to 392. These are findings from my assignment, without external validation; the historical, population-specific data and missing values limit their wider use.

End of module assignment · presentation synopsis

Pima diabetes presentation

My 16-slide presentation takes the analysis from data quality and sample characteristics through group comparisons, regression and limitations. I reported higher mean glucose in the diabetes group (142.3 compared with 110.6) and used the model results to explain why a single accuracy figure is insufficient.

I also examined whether adding a BMI–age interaction improved the logistic model. The likelihood-ratio test was not significant (p = .453), and AIC increased. My conclusion focused on cautious interpretation and further validation, including the trade-off between sensitivity and false positives when choosing a threshold. I keep the complete slides and transcript in my private study archive.

03 / What · So what · What next

Reflective review

Looking back at my coursework using the departmental three-question framework.

What?

In Unit 8, I summarised age, compared diastolic blood pressure by diabetes status and compared systolic blood pressure across occupational groups. In my final assignment, I extended the analysis to data quality, group differences, correlation and regression using the Pima diabetes dataset.

So what?

In my Pima analysis, I distinguished between empty CSV cells and implausible physiological zeros. How I handle those values changes the sample available for modelling, linking data preparation directly to inference. My presentation reports 77.0% classification accuracy alongside 56.8% sensitivity, which shows why I need more than one performance measure. Likewise, my non-significant Unit 8 results do not establish that the groups are identical.

What next?

My next steps are to keep the complete analysis script, package versions and model sample sizes alongside every reported result. I need to report uncertainty and effect sizes as well as p-values, inspect assumptions and compare missing-data strategies. I also need to evaluate predictive performance on held-out data before drawing conclusions about generalisation.
