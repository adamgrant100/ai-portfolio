02 / Coursework summaries

Evidence and artefacts

I summarise my coursework below, alongside selected results and the original model outputs. I keep the complete assignments in my private study archive.

Collaborative discussion · Synopsis

AI adoption and organisational change

I examined why organisations adopt AI, starting with the availability of data, computing power and improved machine-learning methods. Retail and e-commerce provided examples of potential efficiency and revenue benefits. The discussion then broadened my focus to changes in skilled work and the organisational investment needed to make those benefits possible.

The productivity J-curve helped explain why adopting a system does not necessarily produce immediate gains: complementary changes to skills and working practices matter. My main conclusion was that governance, investment in people and management of change belong alongside technical capability when evaluating AI adoption. This was a discussion-based review, rather than a measurement of an organisation’s productivity.

Selected reading: Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson (2021), The productivity J-curve; Leslie (2019), Understanding artificial intelligence ethics and safety.

Unit 2 seminar · Synopsis

Industry 4.0 and Industry 5.0

My seminar presentation compared Industry 4.0’s connected systems, data and automation with Industry 5.0’s emphasis on people, sustainability and resilience. I used industrial AI to examine how technical performance relates to reliability and usefulness in a working environment.

The presentation considered the social, economic, ethical and professional challenges of adoption, including human oversight and the difficulty of moving from pilots to wider use. I argued that explainability, collaboration and responsible design should be part of implementation decisions. The work was a review of the literature and its strategic implications; it did not test an industrial system or establish how well a particular deployment performed.

Selected reading: Peres et al. (2020), Industrial artificial intelligence in Industry 4.0: Systematic review, challenges and outlook.

AI solution implementation · Synopsis

Credit-risk prediction using WEKA

I compared J48 and RandomForest using 30,000 records from UCI’s credit-card default dataset. After removing the ID field, I ran both classifiers in WEKA with default settings and ten-fold cross-validation. The aim was to assess a credit-risk decision-support scenario and compare an interpretable tree with an ensemble model.

RandomForest achieved 81.66% accuracy against J48’s 80.33%, but identified only 37.1% of actual defaults. The majority-class baseline was already 77.88%, so the headline accuracy overstated the practical improvement. I concluded that further evaluation was needed, including error costs, sensitive or proxy features and performance on data representative of the intended users. The historical Taiwanese dataset and weak default recall limit the case for transferring these models to a UK lending context.

Dataset: Yeh (2009), Default of credit card clients, UCI Machine Learning Repository. Selected experimental results and original model outputs follow.

03 / What · So what · What next

Reflective review

Looking back at my coursework using the departmental three-question framework.

What?

In my discussion summary, I described how my understanding of AI adoption moved from efficiency and revenue to organisational change requiring investment in people and governance. In my WEKA project, I applied that perspective to a credit-risk problem by comparing two classifiers.

So what?

My RandomForest results show 81.6633% accuracy, but default-class recall is only 0.371. Against the 77.88% majority-class baseline in my report, headline accuracy alone gives an incomplete picture. I need to consider which errors matter, how the data represent the intended setting, and whether people can inspect and challenge a result. My project supports an initial feasibility judgement; it does not establish that the model is ready for deployment.

What next?

My next steps are to evaluate threshold and error-cost choices, document preprocessing and assess potential proxy features. I need to compare against an explicit baseline and use suitable validation for the intended population, keeping the configuration, data provenance and evaluation outputs so that I can reproduce the comparison.
